Solinas Integrity: IIT Madras Robotics Startup Fixing India's Water Pipes

Robotics · Water Infrastructure · Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

What Is Solinas Integrity?

Solinas Integrity is a Chennai-based robotics startup, incubated at IIT Madras in 2018, that develops AI-powered robots and software for inspecting, cleaning, and managing underground water and wastewater networks. Its systems are already deployed across at least 35 cities and 15 states in India — a genuinely significant operational footprint for a company at this funding stage, suggesting real revenue-generating deployments rather than pilot-only traction.

Business Model

Solinas generates revenue through contracts with municipal bodies and water utilities, deploying its robotic inspection and cleaning systems to identify pipe damage, blockages, and infrastructure issues in underground water and sewage networks — problems that are historically expensive and dangerous to diagnose using manual inspection methods (including, in the worst cases, hazardous manual sewer entry). The company's software layer likely also generates recurring revenue through data/analytics subscriptions tied to infrastructure monitoring over time, rather than one-off inspection fees alone.

Why Water Infrastructure Robotics Matters

India's underground water and sewage infrastructure is extensive, aging in many cities, and historically difficult to inspect and maintain safely. Manual sewer inspection carries serious safety risks that have led to real, documented harm to sanitation workers — making automation in this space not just an efficiency play but a genuine public safety and dignity issue. Companies solving this technically also address a significant municipal governance challenge, since water loss and infrastructure failure are costly, hard-to-diagnose problems for city governments managing aging networks.

Market Position

Solinas operates in a relatively specialized niche — infrastructure robotics for water/wastewater — with limited direct competition in India specifically, though the broader category of infrastructure inspection robotics has global players. The company's plans to expand into the Middle East and Southeast Asia suggest confidence that its India-honed technology and operational model can transfer to other markets with similar aging-infrastructure challenges.

What the Funding Will Support

Per the company, funds will go toward increasing manufacturing capacity, developing new products, and strengthening software infrastructure — alongside plans to enter more Indian states and expand into the Middle East and Southeast Asia.
