Imarticus Learning: ₹800 Crore Acquisition Ahead of a ₹1,000 Crore IPO

Edtech · M&A · Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

Edtech firm Imarticus Learning made its first international acquisition, buying Singapore's BELLS Institute for ₹800 crore, as it prepares for a planned ₹1,000 crore IPO — a notable double milestone for an Indian edtech company in a sector that has faced more scrutiny from investors and public markets in recent years.

What Is Imarticus Learning?

Imarticus Learning is an Indian edtech company focused on professional and career-oriented education — training programs typically aimed at working professionals and job-seekers in areas like finance, analytics, technology, and business skills, rather than the K-12 or test-prep segments that dominate much of India's consumer edtech landscape. This positioning in professional/upskilling education has generally proven more resilient than consumer-facing K-12 edtech, which saw significant valuation corrections and business model challenges in recent years.

The BELLS Institute Acquisition

Imarticus's ₹800 crore acquisition of Singapore-based BELLS Institute marks its first international deal, extending the company's footprint beyond India into Southeast Asia's professional education market. International acquisitions of this scale by an Indian edtech company signal both balance sheet strength and a strategic push to diversify revenue geographically ahead of a public listing — international revenue diversification is often viewed favorably by IPO investors assessing growth durability.

Why the IPO Timing Matters

Imarticus's planned ₹1,000 crore IPO comes at a time when Indian edtech as a sector has faced more cautious investor sentiment following well-publicized struggles at some larger consumer edtech companies. A successful listing from a professional-education-focused player like Imarticus — with a demonstrated ability to make a meaningful international acquisition ahead of listing — could serve as an important signal that public markets are willing to differentiate between edtech sub-segments rather than treating the sector uniformly.

Business Model

Imarticus generates revenue through fee-based professional training and certification programs, typically delivered through a mix of online and in-person formats, targeting working professionals seeking career transitions or skill upgrades in finance, analytics, and technology fields. This B2C-with-clear-ROI model (learners can often directly link program completion to career/salary outcomes) has generally proven more durable than subscription-based consumer learning apps.

Market Position & Competition

Imarticus competes in India's professional upskilling and career education space alongside players like UpGrad and various specialized bootcamp-style providers, though its specific focus on finance and analytics certification, combined with its now-international footprint via the BELLS acquisition, provides some differentiation from broader-focus competitors.

What to Watch as the IPO Process Develops

Key details to watch as Imarticus moves toward its ₹1,000 crore IPO include the fresh issue versus offer-for-sale structure, revenue growth trends post-acquisition, and how the integration of BELLS Institute's Singapore operations progresses ahead of the listing.
