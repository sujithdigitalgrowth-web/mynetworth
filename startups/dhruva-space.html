Dhruva Space: First Company Backed by India's Sovereign Space Fund

Spacetech · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

Dhruva Space became the first recipient of investment from the Antariksh Venture Capital Fund (AVCF) — India's ₹1,600 crore dedicated spacetech fund anchored by IN-SPACe and managed by SIDBI Venture Capital — securing ₹60 crore in what the fund described as a landmark moment for India's space technology ecosystem.

What Is Dhruva Space?

Dhruva Space is a Hyderabad-based spacetech startup, founded in 2012 by Chaitanya Dora Surapureddy, Sanjay Nekkanti, Abhay Egoor, and Krishna Teja Penamakuru. The company builds end-to-end space engineering solutions — including small satellites, ground stations, launch solutions, and mission support — serving commercial, government, and academic customers. It positions itself as a "full-stack" space company spanning manufacturing, mission execution, and commercial deployment.

Business Model

Dhruva Space generates revenue through satellite manufacturing, ground station infrastructure, launch support services, and mission support for a mix of commercial, government, and academic customers — a diversified revenue base across the space value chain rather than dependence on a single product line (like satellite manufacturing alone) or customer type (like pure government contracts).

Why Being AVCF's First Investment Matters

The Antariksh Venture Capital Fund, first announced in the Union Budget 2024, is expected to back around 35 startups over five years — but Dhruva Space becoming its very first investment is a significant credibility signal, both for the company and for India's broader spacetech ecosystem. Government-anchored funds typically apply rigorous selection criteria for their inaugural investments specifically, since that first deal sets a public benchmark for the fund's investment thesis.

Market Position & Competition

Dhruva Space operates in India's growing private spacetech sector alongside companies like Skyroot Aerospace, Agnikul Cosmos, and Bellatrix Aerospace — competitors identified in third-party company analysis of the sector. India's space technology market is projected to reach $77 billion by 2030, driving increased government support through initiatives like AVCF and IN-SPACe's Technology Adoption Fund.

What the Funding Will Support

Capital will support expansion of satellite manufacturing capabilities, development of space infrastructure, advancement of critical technologies, and execution of customer programmes across both domestic and international markets.
